Therapist website design: what a private practice site needs in 2026

People looking for a therapist want to feel understood before they ever book. Here is what a private practice website costs in 2026, what it must say, and how to handle scheduling and intake without risking client privacy.

The short answer

A therapist website usually costs anywhere from a low monthly fee on a template platform to $2,000 to $10,000 for a custom design, depending on the number of pages, specialties and locations. Most solo practices need five to eight pages, a clear way to book a free consultation, and a link to a secure client portal for intake and scheduling.

Your website does not need to hold sensitive data itself. Let a practice management tool that signs a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) handle intake forms, records and payments, and use the website to build trust and explain how you work.

Key takeaways

Speak to one kind of client per specialty page, in their words, not in clinical jargon. Show your face, fees, insurance and availability, because uncertainty stops people from booking. Never collect health information through a standard contact form; route it through a HIPAA-compliant portal instead.

The pages every private practice website needs

  • Home page. Who you help, where (in person, online, which states or countries you are licensed in) and one clear action: "Book a free 15-minute consultation".
  • About page. Your photo, training, licence, approach and why you do this work. People choose a person, not a practice.
  • Specialty pages. One page per focus area, such as anxiety, couples therapy, trauma or teen therapy. Describe what clients feel and what working with you looks like. These pages also rank for searches like "anxiety therapist in [city]".
  • Fees and insurance. Session fee, sliding scale if you offer one, insurance you accept, superbills, and your cancellation policy. In the US, include the Good Faith Estimate notice if it applies to you.
  • How it works. What happens from the first call to the first session, how long sessions last, and what online therapy looks like.
  • Contact and booking. A link to your secure portal for booking, plus a simple contact option that asks for no clinical details.
  • Crisis notice. A short line that you do not offer emergency services, with the local emergency number and crisis line.

Booking, intake forms and privacy

In the US, HIPAA applies to most therapists who bill insurance electronically, and many others follow it as a standard. Any vendor that stores or handles client health information for you needs to sign a BAA. Most standard website form tools and email newsletters will not, so they are the wrong place for intake questions. Outside the US, rules such as GDPR treat health data as a special category with stricter requirements.

The safe pattern is simple: the website explains and invites, the portal collects.

Practice management tools built for therapists, such as SimplePractice (plans from about $49/month as of late 2026), include a client portal and online booking you can link to or embed. Confirm the BAA in writing with any vendor you use. We are not lawyers, so ask your professional association or a compliance adviser if you are unsure.

Design that feels calm and safe

A therapist site should lower anxiety, not add to it. In practice that means:

  • Warm, real photos of you and your office instead of generic stock images of people holding their heads.
  • Plain language. "Feeling stuck in the same arguments" lands better than "relational dysfunction".
  • Calm colours and generous spacing, with strong contrast so text stays readable.
  • One action per page. Do not make a nervous visitor choose between five buttons.
  • Accessibility. Readable font sizes, keyboard-friendly navigation and alt text for images.
  • Fast mobile pages. Many people search for help late at night on their phone.

How clients find therapists online

Most private practices get clients from three places, and a website supports all of them.

  1. Directories. Psychology Today, TherapyDen, GoodTherapy and local directories send referrals. Link each profile to your website so people can learn more before they book.
  2. Google search and Maps. Claim your Google Business Profile, keep your name, address and phone the same everywhere, and write specialty pages for your city. If you only work online, focus on the states or regions where you are licensed.
  3. AI search. People now ask ChatGPT, Claude and Google AI Overviews for therapist recommendations. Clear pages that say who you help, where, how much it costs and how to book are easier for these tools to understand and quote.

Referrals from doctors, schools and other therapists also land on your website first, so it should confirm in seconds that you are the right fit.

Checklist before you launch

  • Licence, credentials and the regions you can practise in are stated clearly
  • Fees, insurance and cancellation policy are on the site
  • Booking and intake run through a portal from a vendor that has signed a BAA
  • The contact form asks for no clinical details
  • Crisis notice on the contact page and in the footer
  • Each specialty page has a unique title, description and local wording
  • Google Business Profile and directory profiles link to the website
  • Pages load fast on a phone and pass basic accessibility checks

Template or custom: how to choose

A template works when you are starting out and need to be bookable this month. Choose a custom site when you have a clear niche, run a group practice, want to rank for competitive local searches, or simply want a site that sounds and looks like you.

How much does a therapist website cost?

From a small monthly fee on a template platform to about $2,000 to $10,000 for a custom solo practice site. Group practices with several clinicians usually cost more; Sorvel websites start at $6,900.

Does my therapy website need to be HIPAA compliant?

The website itself should not collect health information. Use a practice management tool that signs a Business Associate Agreement for intake, records and payments, and keep the website form to name, contact and preferred time.

Is a Psychology Today profile enough?

It is a good source of referrals, at $29.95 a month as of late 2026, but you do not own the page and you appear next to competitors. A website gives you your own space, ranks for your specialties and supports every directory profile.

What pages should a therapist website have?

A home page, about page, one page per specialty, fees and insurance, how it works, and a contact or booking page with a crisis notice.

Should I list my fees on my website?

Yes, in most cases. Clear fees, insurance details and a sliding scale if you offer one remove a major reason people hesitate to book.

Can I add online booking to my therapist website?

Yes. Link to or embed the booking widget from your practice management software, so availability, reminders and intake stay in one secure system.

How long does it take to build a therapist website?

A template can be live in days. A custom Sorvel landing page takes 1 to 2 weeks and a full website usually 3 to 6 weeks, depending on content.
