How much a Framer website costs in 2026

Framer has become one of the fastest ways for startups and design-led brands to launch a polished, motion-rich website. Here is what it costs to do it properly, and what to watch for.

Mykola TanasiichukFounder, Sorvel · 1 November 2026

The short answer

A custom Framer website built by a professional team usually costs $3,000 to $15,000 in 2026 and takes 2 to 5 weeks. A single Framer landing page usually costs $1,500 to $5,000.

With our studio a Framer website starts from $6,900 and a Framer landing page from $2,400. On top of that, Framer charges a monthly site plan; check Framer's pricing page for current fees.

Key takeaways

Framer is usually a little faster and cheaper to build than Webflow for the same design. The price follows the number of unique layouts and the amount of custom motion, not the number of pages.

Some freelancers design directly in Framer and skip Figma. That can be cheaper for very small sites, but a Figma design makes it easier to agree on the look before the build and to reuse the design later.

What drives the price

  • Unique layouts. Home, product, pricing, about and blog post templates each take design and build time. Extra pages on an existing layout are cheap.
  • Motion. Subtle fades are quick. Scroll-driven product stories and interactive components take days.
  • CMS. A simple blog is easy. Several linked collections, such as case studies, team and integrations, take more planning.
  • Content. Ready copy and images keep the price down. Copywriting, illustration and photography add to it.
  • Languages. Framer supports localisation, but every language needs layout checks.

Costs after launch

  • Framer site plan, billed monthly or yearly, depending on traffic, CMS limits and features.
  • Domain, usually a small yearly fee with your registrar.
  • Tools, such as form handling, analytics or a scheduler, if you use paid versions.
  • Updates. Your team can edit text, images and CMS items itself. New layouts or sections are usually a small paid task or part of a monthly plan.

There are no plugins to update and no servers to maintain, which is where Framer and Webflow save money compared with WordPress over a year.

When Framer is the right choice, and when it is not

Choose Framer ifConsider Webflow or WordPress if
You need to launch in weeksYou publish a lot of content every week

Mistakes that make Framer sites expensive

  • Starting the build before the content and structure are agreed.
  • Adding heavy motion everywhere, which slows the site and the project.
  • Choosing Framer for a site that will soon need a large CMS.
  • Skipping mobile design and fixing it after launch.
