How to write a website brief, with a free template

A clear brief gets you accurate quotes, fewer revisions and a better website. It does not need to be long, just specific.

Mykola TanasiichukFounder, Sorvel · 1 November 2026

The short answer

A good website brief answers seven questions: who you are, who the site is for, what visitors should do, which pages you need, what content you already have, which sites you like and why, and your budget and deadline.

Copy-ready brief template

  1. About us: what we do in one or two sentences.
  2. Audience: who we sell to and what they care about.
  3. Goal: the main action, such as demo requests, sales or calls.
  4. Pages: list of page types, for example home, services, pricing, case studies, blog, contact.
  5. Content: what is ready (copy, photos, logo) and what we need help with.
  6. References: two or three websites we like, and what we like about them.
  7. Platform and integrations: Webflow, Framer, WordPress or open; CRM, booking, analytics.
  8. Budget and timeline: a range and a launch date.

Common brief mistakes

  • Listing features without saying what the site should achieve.
  • No budget range, which makes quotes impossible to compare.
  • "Modern and clean" as the only design direction. Show examples instead.

Summary

In one minute

A brief does not need to be long. Answer the eight questions in the template, attach two or three reference sites and give a budget range. That is enough for any good team to give you an accurate fixed quote.

A filled-in example

  1. About us: B2B software that helps restaurants track licences and staff training.
  2. Audience: owners and operations managers of restaurant groups in the US.
  3. Goal: demo requests.
  4. Pages: home, features, pricing, about, contact, blog.
  5. Content: logo and product screenshots ready; homepage copy needs help.
  6. References: two SaaS sites we like for their clear product screens.
  7. Platform: Webflow; HubSpot forms.
  8. Budget and timeline: $7,000 to $10,000; launch in six weeks.

What content to prepare

  • Logo files and brand colours, if you have them.
  • Product screenshots or photos.
  • Team photos and short bios.
  • Client logos, testimonials and case study notes.
  • Any existing copy, even rough, and the questions customers ask most.

How long should a website brief be?

One or two pages is plenty if it answers the key questions.

Should I include a budget?

Yes. A range helps agencies suggest the right scope instead of guessing.

What if I don't know which pages I need?

No. A rough list of page types is enough.
