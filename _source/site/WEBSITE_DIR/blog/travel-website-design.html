Travel and tourism website design: a practical guide

Travel websites sell a feeling first and a plan second. The best ones move visitors from 'that looks amazing' to 'send me a quote' without friction. Here is how to design that journey for a destination, a tour operator or a travel brand.

Mykola TanasiichukFounder, Sorvel · 1 November 2026

The short answer

A travel or tourism website needs big, honest imagery, a quick way to find the right experience, ready-made itineraries or tours with dates and prices, and a short inquiry or booking form. Most visitors plan on a phone, so mobile comes first.

A custom travel website usually costs $5,000 to $20,000 with a professional team. With our studio it starts from $6,900.

Key takeaways

Inspire, then help people plan. Show dates, duration and price on every tour. Keep the inquiry form short. Use real photos of the place, and design for phones first.

Running holiday homes or villas? Our guide to vacation rental websites with direct booking covers booking engines and calendar sync.

From inspiration to inquiry

  1. Inspire: a strong hero with a real photo or video, the place's story and a few key facts.
  2. Explore: filters by interest, such as diving, culture, family, adventure or wellness.
  3. Plan: itineraries or tours with duration, level, season and price.
  4. Act: a short inquiry or booking form with a clear reply time.

For Visit Pemba, the travel guide to Pemba Island in Zanzibar, we built exactly this path: an aerial hero with four island facts, arched story cards inspired by Swahili doorways, an experience finder by interest and ready-made itineraries leading to a trip inquiry. It was designed in Figma and built in WordPress.

Details that build confidence

  • Best time to visit and what each season is like.
  • How to get there, with travel times.
  • Named reviews with the trip and date.
  • Clear cancellation and payment terms.
  • Responsible travel and local partners, if they matter to your audience.

Booking or inquiry?

Design for planning on a phone

Travellers browse on their phones, often in short sessions. Use large images that load fast, swipeable cards for tours, sticky "Plan your trip" buttons and forms with as few fields as possible. Save details like dates and group size for a second step.

Which platform

WordPress suits content-rich destination sites with many pages, tours and booking plugins. Webflow suits design-led tour operators that want an easy CMS without plugin maintenance. Framer works for smaller, highly visual brands. See Webflow vs WordPress.

Common travel website mistakes

  • Stock beach photos that could be anywhere.
  • Tours without dates, duration or price.
  • Long inquiry forms on mobile.
  • Heavy images that make pages slow on mobile networks.
  • No practical information, so visitors leave to find it elsewhere.
