Framer vs WordPress: which is right for your website

Framer and WordPress sit at opposite ends: one is a design tool that publishes websites, the other a flexible system that can become almost anything. Here is how to choose.

Mykola TanasiichukFounder, Sorvel · 3 November 2026

The short answer

Choose Framer for a modern marketing or launch site where design and motion matter, the content is fairly simple and you want zero maintenance.

Choose WordPress when the site needs complex features, a large store, memberships or deep custom logic, and you have someone to maintain it.

When Framer wins

  • You need to launch in weeks, not months.
  • The site is mostly pages, a blog and forms.
  • Your designer wants to update the site directly.
  • You never want to think about plugin updates or hosting again.

When WordPress wins

  • Online store with many products and custom checkout rules.
  • Memberships, courses, bookings or directories.
  • Large content libraries with custom fields and logic.
  • You want full control over code and hosting.

If your content is growing fast but you do not need WordPress features, Webflow is often the middle ground. See Webflow vs Framer and Webflow vs WordPress.

Summary

In one minute

Framer wins on speed of launch, design quality and zero maintenance. WordPress wins on functionality: stores, memberships, complex content and thousands of plugins. If you need neither extreme, Webflow is often the middle ground.

WordPress looks cheaper per month, but maintenance time and plugin licences add up. Framer costs more per month and usually less per year for a marketing site.

Editing day to day

  • Framer: edit text and images directly on the page, add CMS items such as blog posts in a simple panel. Very easy for designers and marketers.
  • WordPress: the block editor or a page builder. Flexible, but layouts can drift as more people edit, and updates sometimes break things.

Moving from WordPress to Framer

  1. List the pages and posts that bring traffic and links.
  2. Design the new site in Figma and rebuild it in Framer.
  3. Import blog content into the Framer CMS.
  4. Set up 301 redirects for every changed URL.
  5. Check Search Console for a few weeks after launch.

The same checklist as in our WordPress to Webflow guide applies.

Framer or WordPress for SEO?

This is the question we hear most, and the honest answer is that both can rank well. Google does not prefer one platform. It ranks pages that load fast, answer the search and have a clean structure. The difference is in how much work it takes to get there.

SEO taskFramerWordPress
Titles and meta descriptionsBuilt in, per page and per CMS itemUsually through a plugin such as Yoast or Rank Math
Sitemap and robots.txtGenerated automaticallyGenerated by core or an SEO plugin
301 redirectsSet in site settingsPlugin or server rules
Structured data (schema)Added as custom code per pagePlugins generate most of it automatically
Page speedFast out of the box, served from a CDNDepends on hosting, theme, plugins and caching

Framer is the easier choice for a company site with a blog: speed and clean markup come by default, and there is no plugin stack to slow pages down. WordPress gives more control when SEO depends on thousands of pages, complex categories, faceted filters or programmatic pages.

What actually moves rankings

On either platform, rankings come from useful content, a clear page structure, internal links and links from other sites. The platform only decides how easy the technical part is. A fast Framer page with thin content will not beat a slower WordPress page that answers the question better.

Security: is Framer safer than WordPress?

For a typical marketing site, Framer carries far less security risk, mostly because there is much less for you to manage.

  • Framer is a hosted platform. Framer runs the servers, SSL certificates and updates. There are no plugins or themes on your side to patch, and no admin login on your domain for bots to attack.
  • WordPress core is well maintained, but most real-world problems come from outdated plugins, themes and weak admin passwords. Security depends on regular updates, backups, a good host and often a security plugin.

That does not make WordPress unsafe. It means WordPress security is a job someone has to do every month. If nobody on your team owns that job, Framer or Webflow removes the risk instead of managing it.

One thing to weigh on the other side: with Framer your site lives on Framer's platform. You cannot move the code to your own server. WordPress is open source, so you can host it anywhere and take everything with you.

Speed and Core Web Vitals

Framer publishes pre-built pages to a global CDN, compresses images and handles caching for you, so a well-built Framer site usually scores well on Core Web Vitals without extra work. Heavy animations and very large images can still slow it down, so they need the same care as anywhere else.

WordPress can be just as fast, but it takes deliberate choices: good managed hosting, a light theme, few plugins, a caching layer and optimised images. Many slow WordPress sites are slow because plugins were added over the years and never removed.

Multilingual sites

  • Framer has built-in localization: you translate pages and CMS items inside the editor, and each language gets its own URL for search engines.
  • WordPress uses plugins such as WPML or Polylang. They are powerful and handle complex setups, but they add another plugin to keep updated and can slow the editor down on big sites.

For two to five languages on a company site, Framer is simpler. For many languages with different content per market, WordPress or Webflow give more room.

Design freedom and animations

Framer was built by people who make design tools, and it shows. Layouts, hover effects, scroll animations and page transitions are set visually, very close to how a designer works in Figma. What you approve in the design is what goes live.

On WordPress the result depends on the theme or page builder and on the developer. Anything is possible with custom code, but advanced motion takes more development time, and page builders often add extra code that slows the page down.

A quick way to decide

Answer these five questions. If most answers point one way, that is your platform.

  1. Do you sell many products online? Yes: WordPress with WooCommerce, or Shopify. No: Framer works.
  2. Do you need logins, memberships, courses or bookings? Yes: WordPress. No: Framer.
  3. Will you publish more than a few hundred articles with complex categories? Yes: WordPress or Webflow. No: Framer is enough.
  4. Is there someone to update plugins and watch security every month? No: Framer.
  5. Is design and first impression the main goal of the site? Yes: Framer.

Is Framer or WordPress better for SEO?

Both can rank well. Framer is faster to set up correctly and fast by default; WordPress gives more control for very large content sites with complex categories.

Is a Framer website secure?

Yes. Framer hosts the site and handles servers, SSL and updates, so there are no plugins or admin logins on your domain to attack. WordPress can be secure too, but it needs regular updates and backups.

Can I export my Framer site and host it myself?

No. Framer sites are hosted by Framer. If owning and moving the code matters to you, WordPress or a custom build is the better fit.

Does Framer support multiple languages?

Yes. Framer has built-in localization for pages and CMS content, with a separate URL for each language.

Is Framer good for SEO?

Yes for most marketing sites: clean pages, fast loading, editable meta tags and sitemaps. Very large content sites get more control on Webflow or WordPress.

Is Framer cheaper than WordPress?

Framer has a monthly plan but no maintenance costs. WordPress hosting is cheap, but updates, plugins and fixes add up over a year.

Can I move from WordPress to Framer?

Yes. Pages are rebuilt in Framer, blog content is imported and old URLs get 301 redirects to keep rankings.

Do you build on both?

Yes. We design in Figma and build in Framer, Webflow or WordPress depending on what the site needs.

How much does a Framer website cost?

Usually $3,000 to $15,000 with a professional team; see our Framer website cost guide for the breakdown.

Is WordPress better for large blogs?

For very large content libraries with custom logic, yes. For a normal company blog, Framer or Webflow is enough.
