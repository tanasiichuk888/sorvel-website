How much a landing page costs in 2026

A landing page has one job: turn a specific visitor into a lead, a sign-up or a sale. What you pay depends on how much strategy, design, building and testing goes into that one job. Here is the full picture, with real numbers.

The short answer

A professionally designed and built landing page usually costs $1,500 to $6,000 in 2026. A template you set up yourself can cost almost nothing, and a page from a conversion agency with ongoing testing can cost $5,000 or more every month.

With our studio a landing page starts from $2,400, each extra page on the same design from $600, and it is usually live in 1 to 2 weeks.

Key takeaways

You pay for strategy and conversion work, not for the number of sections. A cheap page that does not convert is the most expensive option once you add up ad spend. Always ask what is included: copy, integrations, tracking and revisions.

The ranges overlap because "landing page" means very different things. A one-screen waitlist page and a long sales page for a $10,000 service are both landing pages, but the second needs far more thinking, writing and proof.

Where the money goes: a cost breakdown

For a typical studio landing page, the budget is roughly split like this:

TaskShare of budgetWhat it covers
Strategy and structure15 to 20%Audience, offer, objections, section order, the one action
Copy and messaging10 to 20%Headline, proof points, calls to action (if not supplied)
Design30 to 35%Desktop and mobile layouts in Figma, visuals, product scenes
Build25 to 30%Webflow, Framer or WordPress, responsive, fast, editable
Integrations and QA5 to 10%Forms, CRM, analytics, ad pixels, testing on devices

When a quote is much lower than the market, one of these lines is usually missing. Most often it is strategy and QA, which are exactly the parts that decide whether the page converts and whether your tracking works.

What moves the price up or down

  • Copy. Ready, tested copy keeps the price down. Writing from scratch adds time, especially for complex B2B offers.
  • Length and proof. A short page for a free trial needs less than a long page that sells a high-ticket service with case studies, pricing and FAQ.
  • Custom visuals. Product scenes, illustrations and motion take longer than photography and simple layouts.
  • Integrations. HubSpot, Salesforce, booking tools, payment links and event tracking each add setup and testing.
  • Variants. Different pages for each ad campaign or audience are cheaper when they share one design system.
  • Languages. A second language means a second round of layout checks, not only translation.
  • Platform. Framer and Webflow are usually faster to build than a custom WordPress theme.

Hidden costs to plan for

  • Platform plan. Webflow and Framer charge a monthly site plan. Check their current pricing pages.
  • Tools. Form tools, schedulers, CRM seats and analytics can add a small monthly cost.
  • Ad spend. The page is only half of a campaign. A page that converts at 4% instead of 2% halves your cost per lead.
  • Changes after launch. New offers and tests are easier if the page is built on reusable components your team can edit.

How to judge if a landing page is worth it

Work backwards from your numbers. If you spend $3,000 a month on ads and the page converts at 2%, moving it to 3% gives you 50% more leads for the same budget. For most businesses running paid traffic, a well-built page pays for itself within the first one or two months.

If you have no traffic plan yet, start smaller: one strong page, a clear offer and basic tracking. Then improve it with real data instead of guessing.

How a landing page project runs

  1. Brief. You share the offer, audience, traffic source and examples you like.
  2. Structure. A section-by-section outline, agreed before design starts.
  3. Design. Desktop and mobile layouts in Figma, with one round of feedback built in.
  4. Build. The page goes live in Webflow, Framer or WordPress with forms and tracking connected.
  5. Launch and check. Speed, forms, pixels and mobile tested before traffic arrives.

A page built this way is a good base for A/B tests later. See our landing pages service.

Checklist before you ask for quotes

  • What is the single action you want visitors to take?
  • Where will traffic come from: ads, email, social or search?
  • Do you have copy, or do you need it written?
  • Which tools must the form connect to?
  • Two or three landing pages you like, and why.
  • Your budget range and launch date.

Common mistakes that waste budget

  • Sending ad traffic to the homepage instead of a page built for that offer.
  • Several competing calls to action on one page.
  • A desktop-only design when most ad clicks come from phones.
  • No conversion tracking, so nobody knows what works.
  • Choosing the cheapest quote and rebuilding the page three months later.

Hourly rates vs a fixed price

Many people search for landing page setup rates, because some designers quote by the hour. Typical rates in 2026 look like this:

Hourly pricing looks flexible, but the final bill is hard to predict, and every round of changes adds hours. A fixed price agreed after the brief is easier to plan: you know the total, the scope and the timeline before work starts. That is how we quote every landing page.

What a landing page setup includes

The design is only part of the job. A page that is ready for traffic also needs a technical setup. Check that your quote covers it:

  • Domain or subdomain connected, with SSL.
  • Hosting on the platform plan, Webflow, Framer or WordPress.
  • Form connected to your CRM, email tool or booking calendar.
  • Analytics and conversion tracking: Google Analytics, Google Ads or Meta pixel, and events on every button that matters.
  • SEO basics: title, description, social sharing image and a clean URL.
  • Speed and mobile checks before launch, because most ad clicks come from phones.
  • Cookie banner if you run ads to visitors in the EU or the UK.

Setup on its own usually takes half a day to two days, so it adds a few hundred dollars when it is quoted separately. Ours is included in the price.

If you plan several campaigns, ask for a reusable template. The first page costs more, and every next page on the same system costs a fraction of it.

Should you build it yourself?

A template is fine to test an idea for a week. It stops being cheap when real ad money goes to it. If you spend $2,000 a month on ads and a better page lifts conversion from 2% to 3%, you get 50% more leads for the same spend. Over a few months that pays for a professional page many times over.

Build it yourself when the budget is very small, the offer is still changing every week, or you only need a page for a short test. Hire help when the page carries real traffic, the brand matters or you need tracking you can trust.
