Work/College Classics

Fashion · Varsity jackets · Made in England

Forty-four jackets, one fit.

College Classics is the ready-to-wear line of an English workshop that has made custom varsity jackets since 2011. We designed the store in Figma and built it on Shopify in 28 days: an editorial look that sells a £245 jacket without a sales call, and a colourway system that keeps 44 combinations easy to browse.

The challenge

A maker since 2011. A shop from day one.

For fourteen years the workshop made varsity jackets to order for universities, clubs and companies. Every sale started with a conversation. College Classics is the first jacket with their own name in the collar: finished, boxed and ready to ship.

That meant a store that had to do the selling on its own. It needed to show 44 colourways without turning into a wall of thumbnails, explain why a jacket costs £245, and feel like a heritage brand on the first visit, not a new shop nobody has heard of.

  • Sell online with no sales call
  • Make 44 colourways easy to browse
  • Earn trust for a brand-new name

44 colourways

Eight body colours and a choice of sleeves. Shown the usual way, that's a dropdown nobody opens or a grid nobody finishes.

A price to explain

£245 is fair for 24oz melton and full-grain leather. But the quality doesn't show in a thumbnail, so the page has to explain it.

From custom to ready-made

Fourteen years of reputation sat with the custom business. The new brand needed to borrow that trust without confusing the two.

How we worked

28 days, three milestones, one launch.

The project ran in three fixed milestones. Each one ended with something the client could open, click through and approve before the next began.

Direction

Heritage menswear, not streetwear

We agreed on a look before drawing a single page: cream and black, a heavy slab serif and calm product photography, closer to a classic outfitter than a hype drop.

The idea

Pick a body, pick a sleeve.

Every colourway is a separate Shopify product with its own photos, URL and stock. On the page they act like one: swatches jump between them instantly, and the collection can be filtered by body or sleeve colour. Shoppers get a simple choice, Google gets 44 indexable pages.

Colourways

The whole range, on one screen.

Right under the hero, every colourway sits in a single swatch grid, split body and sleeve like the real jacket. One tap opens that exact product, so people who know what they want skip the browsing.

Product

Built like the ones they don't make any more.

The featured jacket gets a full spec table: body, sleeves, lining, trims and where it's made. One price for all 44 colourways, and delivery, returns and sizes repeated right under the buttons.

Price

Why it costs what it costs.

Instead of discount banners, three plain cards explain the price: the 24oz wool, the full-grain leather and the in-house making. Things that never show in a photo, said out loud.

Story

Fourteen years of borrowed trust.

A new name needs a history. The story block tells how the workshop spent fourteen years making jackets for other people, next to the bear mascot drawn for the brand, so the shop feels established from day one.

Mobile

Most orders start on a phone.

Product photo first, headline second, two full-width buttons and the key facts in one row. The swatch grid scrolls sideways, so all 44 colourways stay one thumb away.

Key decisions

Choices that sell a £245 jacket without a salesperson.

How do you show 44 colourways?

The usual answer

One product with a long colour dropdown.

What we did

44 products linked by swatches, plus body and sleeve colour filters on the collection.

How do you defend the price?

The usual answer

Sale badges and countdown timers.

What we did

One honest price and a section that explains the wool, the leather and the making.

What should it look like?

The usual answer

A stock streetwear theme with loud banners.

What we did

An editorial layout in cream and black, a heavy slab serif and lots of air around the product.

How does a new brand earn trust?

The usual answer

Launch and wait for reviews.

What we did

Lead with the 14-year maker story, Google reviews and "made in England" in the first screen.
