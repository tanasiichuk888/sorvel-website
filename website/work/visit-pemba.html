Work/Visit Pemba

Tourism · Pemba Island · Zanzibar, Tanzania

An island website as beautiful as the island.

Visit Pemba is the travel guide for Pemba Island in the Zanzibar archipelago. We designed a website that sells the island through its landscapes, local stories and ready-made itineraries, and makes sending a trip inquiry the natural next step.

Visit Pemba

Visit Pemba · Figma + WordPress

The challenge

Most travellers have never heard of Pemba.

Pemba sits next to Zanzibar but gets a fraction of the visitors. The website had to introduce the island, make people want to go and help them plan a trip.

It also had to reflect what makes Pemba different: untouched nature, a living Swahili culture and responsible tourism.

  • Sell the island visually
  • Make planning easy
  • Turn interest into trip inquiries

An unknown destination

The site had to answer 'why Pemba?' within the first screen.

Many kinds of traveller

Divers, families, culture lovers and honeymooners all needed a way in.

From dream to plan

Inspiration had to lead to itineraries and a simple inquiry form.

Stories

Stories that make the island unforgettable.

Arched photo cards for clove farms, coral reefs, seaweed farmers and traditional dhows, inspired by Swahili doorways.

Discovery

Find your kind of Pemba.

Filter tabs for marine, nature, culture, community and wellness experiences, each with duration and price.

Planning

Ready-made itineraries.

Two- and four-day plans with clear durations, so visitors can picture the whole trip and ask for it.

Mobile

Made for planning on a phone.

Big imagery, swipeable cards and a short inquiry form, because most travellers plan on mobile.

Key decisions

Small choices that shape the page.

How do you introduce the island?

The usual answer

A slideshow of beaches.

What we did

A full-bleed aerial shot with four facts: dive sites, community focus, flight time and no mass tourism.

How do people explore?

The usual answer

A long list of tours.

What we did

Experience tabs by interest, then ready-made itineraries.

What does it feel like?

The usual answer

Generic resort turquoise.

What we did

Deep teal, warm sand and an editorial serif, with arches taken from local architecture.
