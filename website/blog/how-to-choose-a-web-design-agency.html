How to choose a web design agency: 12 questions to ask

Most website projects that go wrong could have been spotted before they started. A few good questions, asked early, show you who will deliver and who will disappear halfway. Here is how to choose with confidence.

Mykola TanasiichukFounder, Sorvel · 1 November 2026

The short answer

Choose the agency that shows relevant live work, explains a clear process, gives you a written fixed scope and price, and tells you exactly who will do the work. Price matters, but unclear scope is what usually makes a website project expensive.

Key takeaways

Shortlist two or three teams, send them the same brief, and compare written proposals line by line. Ask to see live sites, not only mockups. Make sure you own the domain, files and accounts.

Where to find good options

  • Referrals from founders or marketers you trust.
  • Directories with verified reviews, such as Clutch or the Webflow Experts directory.
  • Freelance platforms like Upwork and Fiverr Pro, where reviews come from real paid projects.
  • Sites you admire. Many sites credit their studio in the footer.

12 questions to ask before you hire

  1. Can I see live websites you built? Mockups are easy to polish. Live sites show real build quality and speed.
  2. Have you worked in my industry or with similar buyers? Not essential, but it shortens the learning curve.
  3. Who designs and who builds my site day to day? Make sure the senior person you talk to is involved in the work.
  4. What exactly is included in the price? Pages, copy, images, SEO setup, integrations, training.
  5. How many revision rounds are included? And what happens if you need more.
  6. What is the timeline, and what do you need from us? Most delays come from missing content and slow feedback.
  7. Which platform do you recommend, and why? A good answer depends on how your team will use the site.
  8. Will my team be able to edit the site without you? Ask to see the editor.
  9. How do you handle SEO, speed and accessibility? Especially redirects if you are replacing an existing site.
  10. Who owns the design files, domain and accounts? The answer should be: you.
  11. What happens after launch? Support, fixes, training and the cost of small changes.
  12. Where can I read verified client reviews? Reviews on platforms with real transactions are harder to fake.

What a fair budget looks like

For a custom business website with a professional team, plan roughly $5,000 to $20,000. A single landing page usually costs $1,500 to $6,000. Large sites with many teams and languages can cost much more. See what a Webflow website costs and what a landing page costs.

Red flags

  • No written scope, or a price that changes after the first conversation.
  • Only mockups and no live sites.
  • Vague answers about who does the work.
  • Pressure to sign quickly with a big upfront payment.
  • Keeping your domain, hosting or accounts in their name.
  • No questions about your business, only about the design you want.

Agency, studio or freelancer?

A large agency suits big projects that need many specialists. A freelancer suits small, clear jobs. A small senior studio sits in between: agency-level quality with one accountable lead. See agency vs freelancer for the full comparison.

How many agencies should I talk to?

Two or three is usually enough to compare scope, price and fit.

Is the cheapest quote a risk?

Often. Very low quotes usually leave out content, SEO, redirects or testing.

Should I choose a local agency?

Not necessarily. Remote teams with a clear process and good reviews work well for most website projects.

What should a website contract include?

Scope, timeline, price, payment stages, revisions, ownership and what happens after launch.

How long does choosing an agency take?

One to two weeks is typical: shortlist, brief, proposals and a decision.
