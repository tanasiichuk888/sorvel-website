How much does a SaaS website cost in 2026?

A SaaS website can cost almost nothing or more than your first engineer. The difference is rarely the number of pages. It is how much thinking goes into what the site says, and how well it shows the product.

Mykola TanasiichukFounder, Sorvel · 25 October 2026

The short answer

For a custom-designed SaaS marketing site of 5 to 10 pages, built in Webflow or Framer so your team can edit it, expect to pay from about $6,900 with a senior studio, delivered in 3 to 6 weeks. Templates cost less but look like everyone else. Large agencies cost many times more and take months.

The rough ranges below come from the market as it looks in 2026. Your own quote depends on the factors further down.

The jump in price between options is mostly about who does the thinking. With a template, you write the story and make every decision. With a studio or agency, someone experienced shapes the messaging, the page structure and the product visuals with you.

What drives the cost

Unique page layouts

A home page, a product page, pricing, a use case, an integration page and a blog post are six layouts. Once each one exists, new pages on the same layout are cheap. Count layouts, not pages.

Product visuals

Generic illustrations are cheap and forgettable. Clean, simplified scenes of your real interface take more work but explain the product in seconds, which is the main job of a SaaS homepage.

Messaging and copy

If you already know what to say and to whom, the project is faster. If positioning is still fuzzy, budget time for it. A beautiful site with an unclear headline converts worse than a plain one that is clear.

CMS and content types

Blog, changelog, customer stories, integrations directory, docs links, careers. Each content type is a collection and a template. Start with the ones you will actually update.

Motion and interactions

Subtle scroll and hover motion is affordable. Heavy custom animation, 3D or interactive product demos are where budgets grow quickly.

Integrations

Demo booking, CRM forms, product sign-up, analytics events and cookie consent. Each is small, but they add up and need testing.

Which pages you need at launch

You don't need everything on day one. A SaaS site that converts well at launch usually has:

  • Home, which explains what the product does, for whom, and shows it;
  • Product or features, the core workflows with real screens;
  • Pricing, even if it says "talk to us";
  • One or two use cases for your main types of buyer;
  • Demo or sign-up, with as few fields as possible;
  • Legal pages such as privacy and terms.

Blog, comparisons, integrations and customer stories can follow once the core pages work. Built on the same components, they are quick to add.

Running costs after launch

  • Hosting. A Webflow or Framer site plan, from around $15 to $40 a month for most marketing sites, depending on the plan and CMS size. Check current prices on the platform's site, as plans changed in 2026.
  • Tools. Analytics, forms or a CRM you probably already pay for.
  • Updates. New landing pages, a pricing change, a feature launch. Your team can do most of it in the CMS. For regular design work, a monthly design plan is usually cheaper than one-off projects.

How not to overpay

  • Fix the message first. Writing or briefing the homepage story before design saves rounds of revisions.
  • Count layouts, not pages. Ask for a quote based on templates, then add pages yourself in the CMS.
  • Show the real product. One accurate product scene beats ten abstract illustrations.
  • Pick a platform your team can use. Webflow suits teams that publish a lot. Framer suits fast, motion-rich sites with fewer content types.
  • Get it in writing. A fixed scope, timeline and price before work starts is the easiest way to avoid surprise invoices.

How long does it take to build a SaaS website?

Usually 3 to 6 weeks for 5 to 10 pages with a senior studio, depending on how ready the content is and how fast feedback comes.

Webflow or Framer for a SaaS website?

Webflow if marketing publishes a lot and needs a strong CMS for blog, changelog or integrations. Framer if you want a fast, motion-rich site with fewer content types.

Can we start small and grow the site later?

Yes, and it is usually the smart way. Launch the core pages on a component system, then add pages on the same building blocks.

Do we need a brand before the website?

It helps, but it is not required. A logo, colours and type can be designed together with the site so they match from the start.

Is it cheaper to use a template?

Upfront, yes. But templates rarely fit your product story, and many teams pay for a redesign within a year. A template is a good choice before product-market fit.
